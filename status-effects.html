    SAMPLE HISTORY

    How the numbers work

    • Blood alcohol (BAC). Shown as a percentage: grams of alcohol per 100 ml of blood. In Canada, 0.08% is the criminal driving limit and most provinces issue roadside suspensions from 0.05%.
    • Canadian standard drink. 13.45 g of alcohol: a 341 ml bottle of 5% beer, a 142 ml glass of 12% wine, or a 43 ml (1.5 oz) shot of 40% spirits.
    • Grams, not drinks. Each drink becomes grams of alcohol: ml × ABV × 0.789. Standard drinks are shown for your region.
    • Absorption lag. On an empty stomach a drink peaks in about 30–45 min. Food in the last 3 hours slows that to 60–90 min and cuts what reaches your blood by about 15%.
    • Your volume. Your weight, height, age and sex estimate your body water, which sets how far each drink is diluted.
    • Fixed recovery. Blood alcohol falls about 0.015% an hour. Water, coffee and sleep don't speed it up.
    • Health bar. Six equal slices between the milestones 0.02, 0.05, 0.08, 0.11, 0.15 and 0.25%, so early drinks are visible.
    • Next-day debuffs come from total grams per kg of body weight, your peak, and how much alcohol is still in your system at bedtime.

    Where each effect comes from

    Verify before launch
    • Impaired sleep

      Alcohol still in your system at bedtime suppresses REM sleep and breaks up the second half of the night, more so at higher doses.

      Ebrahim et al., 2013, Alcoholism: Clinical and Experimental Research
    • HRV drain

      Overnight heart-rate variability recovery fell about 9%, 24% and 39% at low, moderate and high intake per kg of body weight. What is HRV?

      Pietilä et al., 2018, JMIR Mental Health
    • Hangover

      Hangovers peak as blood alcohol returns to zero and become much more common after a peak above about 0.11%.

      Verster and colleagues, Alcohol Hangover Research Group
    • Dehydration

      Alcohol suppresses vasopressin, the hormone that helps your kidneys hold onto water.

      Hobson and Maughan, 2010, Alcohol and Alcoholism
    • Hangxiety

      Next-day anxiety as the brain rebounds from alcohol's calming effect.

      Marsh et al., 2019, Personality and Individual Differences
    • Slow recovery

      Heavy drinking after exercise cut muscle protein synthesis by roughly a quarter to a third.

      Parr et al., 2014, PLoS One
    • Fat burning paused

      The body clears alcohol first; whole-body fat burning dropped sharply after a moderate dose.

      Siler et al., 1999, American Journal of Clinical Nutrition
    • Munchies

      Alcohol increases appetite and food intake at the next meal, often called the apéritif effect.

      Yeomans, 2010, Physiology and Behavior
    • Model inputs

      Body water estimate, elimination rate and the effect of food on absorption.

      Watson et al., 1980, American Journal of Clinical Nutrition · Jones, 2010, Forensic Science International · Jones and Jönsson, 1994, Journal of Forensic Sciences
